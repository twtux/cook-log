# Brines Reference 🧂

## ⚠️ THE SALT RULE (read first)
**ONE salt step, and go light.** Never stack salt.
- If you **wet-brine** → add **ZERO** extra salt after (the brine already seasons through). Season with **salt-free** rub only (e.g. Badia Herbes de Provence).
- If you **dry-brine** → ~**½ tsp kosher salt per lb**, and **skip the wet brine**.
- **A store rub is already the salt** — don't salt/brine on top of it.
- **Skip brining "enhanced" / injected / self-basting** poultry (check the package — it's pre-salted).
- This is *the* fix for the over-salty chicken: don't mix salt steps.

## Overnight Chicken Brine (1 whole bird)

**Water:** 1 gallon cold water

**Salt — PICK EXACTLY ONE (do not combine):**
- **⅔ cup Morton kosher salt**, *or*
- **1 cup + 2 Tbsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt**, *or*
- **½ cup table salt**

_(These three are equal saltiness — different brands/grains just measure differently. Choose the salt you actually have and use only that line.)_

**Aromatics:**
- ⅓ cup brown sugar, lightly packed
- 4 garlic cloves, smashed
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 Tbsp peppercorns
- 1 tsp dried thyme
- optional: lemon peel

**Method**
1. Dissolve salt + sugar in the gallon of cold water; add aromatics.
2. Submerge bird, **refrigerate 8–12 hrs** (pull at **8 hrs** if you want it less salty).
3. Discard brine, **do NOT rinse**, **dry thoroughly** (a 2-day air-dry = great skin).
4. Light oil + **salt-free** seasoning (Herbes de Provence; optional paprika for color) → rotisserie.

**If it still comes out too salty:** shorten to 8 hrs, or it was likely an enhanced/pre-injected bird (don't brine those).
