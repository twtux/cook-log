## ▶️ NEXT TIME — Rotisserie Chicken v2 (the improved plan)
*Tonight was run at 275°F (chosen to hit an 8:00 dinner). The v2 below fixes the two weak spots:
soft skin + the fire stall.*

**Fire start (bulletproof):**
- Light a **small chimney (~8–12 briquettes)**, let them go **gray/glowing** (~10–15 min) — don't rush.
- Lay a **half-chimney of unlit** briquettes concentrated **by the Venom fan/front intake**.
- **Dump the hot glowing coals on top** of the unlit bed → guaranteed, fast, controlled fire.
- RULE #0: confirm coals are actually catching (going gray/glowing) before you close the lid.

**Temps (Tom's call — keep it low, crank at the END):**
- Tom likes the low-and-slow, doesn't care about the longer cook. So: **run 275°F the whole way**
  (juicy), then **HARD CRANK the Venom to 425–450°F for the last 15–20 min** to crisp the skin
  (or torch it). This was the original plan tonight — the dying fire is what stole the end-crank.
- Requires a fire that won't fade → hence the chimney-start below. That's the real enabler.

**Setup (unchanged, it worked):**
- Coal ring or concentrated bank + **drip pan centered** under the bird, indirect.
- Truss tight, spit balanced, wireless probe OR spot-check with the ThermoPro pen (breast, thickest).

**Doneness:**
- Pull **breast 160–165 / thigh 175** (or 158–160 after a long hold = safe via time+temp). Rest ~12 min.

**Ingredient / flavor ideas to try next:**
- SALT (Tom, firm): NEVER mix salt steps + go LIGHTER overall. If wet-brining, add ZERO extra salt
  after (brine already seasons through). If dry-brining, ~1/2 tsp kosher/lb and skip the wet brine.
  When in doubt, less — add at the table, not the cook.
- Keep: 2-day air-dry (great skin base) + Herbes de Provence (salt-free) dusting.
- Try: compound herb-**butter under the skin** (flavors meat, skin stays dry/crispy); lemon + garlic +
  fresh thyme/rosemary in the **cavity**; a light SPG or smoked-paprika rub variation.
- One small wood chunk (apple/cherry/pecan) for a light smoke kiss.

## LESSONS LEARNED (the big ones)
0. **TOM'S #1 RULE: it's all about getting the fire started + controlled right off the bat.** ALWAYS confirm every Jealous Devil BOOM bomb is actually flaming AND that the coals around it are catching (going gray/glowing) before closing the lid and walking away. Don't ASSUME the bombs took — tonight they flared, we assumed, the coals never lit → 45-min stall.
0b. **RUN CHICKEN HOTTER — 325–350°F from the start (Tom's insight).** 275 was chosen to stretch
   the cook to an 8:00 dinner, but for a bird that was a mistake: low-and-slow gave soft skin and a
   long stall. Next time run 325–350 for crispy skin + faster cook (would've powered through the fire
   wobbles), and just start later to hit dinner time. Rotisserie chicken wants HEAT.
1. **Fan controllers need a real, concentrated glowing coal bed to work** — don't try to light a spread-out ring off a couple small starters. Light a **chimney** (or a tight concentrated pile with 2–3 bombs) first, get coals actually ashed/glowing, THEN spread + hand off to the Venom. Would've saved ~50 min.
2. **Every lid-open dumps heat and stalls the climb.** Check temp sparingly (~20-min intervals), not every few minutes.
3. **Coals near the fan burn; the far side suffocates** — concentrate fuel where the fan blows.
4. **Wireless meat probe was unreliable** — a simple instant-read pen (ThermoPro) never failed. Recommended upgrades: ThermoWorks (Thermapen ONE / RFX), Combustion Inc, Typhur Sync.
5. Rotisserie doesn't need even/both-side coals — the **spin** carries the bird through a single hot zone evenly.
