## Slicing — cut across the notch
The 5:55a grain notch runs *along* the grain, so slices cross it at ~90°. Work from that corner across the flat at ~¼". Where the point starts overlapping, stop, separate the two muscles at the fat seam, rotate the point a quarter turn, and read the fibers on the freshly exposed face before continuing.

## The temp story (the big lesson)
- **The stall was brutal — ~8h 15m.**
- **Leave-in probe sat at 149–154°F for eight hours** while hand checks with the instant-read in the **thick part of the flat were reading ~30° higher.**
- **Takeaway: the leave-in probe was in the wrong place.** It was parked somewhere cooler (near fat / thinner section / too close to surface), badly under-reading. The meat was further along than the stuck probe suggested.
- Pit held a flat 250 for most of the cook; a couple late spikes/dips near the end (fire management at the tail).

## Fire management & overnight logistics (the real pain points)
- **Charcoal/fire management wasn't dialed in** — had to refill the coals a couple times through the cook. Not a disaster, but it meant babysitting.
- **The long stall killed the cook.** By ~9:30 PM Tom was falling asleep. Fire wasn't holding.
- **~12:31 AM:** pulled it — pit/fire temp had dropped to **~120°F** (fire dying, no more refills).
- **Wrapped it, moved to the OVEN a couple hours** to finish/hold, then **into the fridge ~3:30 AM.**
- So this wasn't a clean 16h20m smoke-to-slice — the back half was an overnight oven save because the fire died and Tom needed sleep.

## Probe suspicion (Tom, follow-up)
- Leave-in held a flat **~151-159°F** through the stall while the instant-read pen showed the flat ~30° hotter. Tom suspects the **thermometer may need replacing.**
- **Action before next cook:** calibrate the leave-in probe — ice water should read 32°F, boiling water 212°F (Lake Mary = sea level, so 212). If it's off >2-3° or sluggish/jumpy → replace. If it nails both → the miss was probe *placement* (fat seam / too shallow), not the probe.

## Next-cook priorities (Tom's own callouts)
1. **#1: proper long-burn fire setup — Minion or snake method.** A ring/snake of unlit coals with a few lit ones migrating through = steady 12-16h burn with NO refills and no babysitting. This is THE fix for the overnight problem.
2. **Beat the stall — wrap it or crank through it.** Don't ride an 8-hour stall overnight. Wrap in butcher paper at ~165-170° to power through in 1-2h, OR bump the pit temp. Either way you're not up at 12:30 AM fighting a dying fire.
3. Wrapping also fixes the too-dark bark + dryness (see Outcome).

## Lessons for next brisket
1. **Probe placement matters more than probe count.** Put the leave-in in the **dead center of the thickest part of the flat**, not the point, not near fat. Cross-check with an instant-read in a couple spots.
2. **Don't trust a single stuck probe through the stall** — a flat line for hours may mean bad placement, not a true 8-hour stall.
3. **No-wrap on a 22" Huntsman at 250 = ~16h for a ~12 lb packer.** Plan the timeline around that (or wrap to push through the stall faster next time if you want to save hours).
4. Trim yield ~1 lb → **save it for a burger grind**, worked great.

## Outcome (Tom's verdict)
- **Bark:** super dark — pushed past deep-mahogany toward too-dark. No-wrap at 250 for 16h builds a LOT of bark; next time either wrap in butcher paper once the bark sets (~internal 165–170, ~4-6h in) to stop it darkening, or run a touch cooler / pull the cook shorter.
- **Tenderness / moisture:** a little **dry.** Fits the story — the misplaced probe read low (149–154) so it likely rode the heat longer than needed, and no-wrap = more moisture loss. Fixes: better probe placement + wrap through the stall + rest wrapped in a cooler 1-2h before slicing.
- **Slicing miss:** forgot to **cut the point the other way.** The point's grain runs ~90° to the flat — after you slice the flat, rotate the point and slice it against ITS grain. Cutting it the same direction = chewy. Easy fix next time.

**Net:** solid cook, two clear tweaks for next brisket — (1) wrap to control bark + fight dryness, (2) rotate the point when slicing. Leftovers reheated Sat 8/15 (low + broth + foil) and trim → burger blend.
