# Flavor Prep Reference 🧄🔥

How the pros build flavor: in **layers**, from deepest (day before) to last (the finish). Each layer is logged in the cook's **Seasoning** field with exact amounts.

---

## The 4 layers

### 1. Deep — brine or injection (day before / hours ahead)
Gets flavor and moisture *inside* the meat, not just on the surface.

- **Dry brine (use for beef + poultry + thick pork):** kosher salt on the bare meat, uncovered in the fridge. Rule: **½ tsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt per lb**, on 12–24 hrs ahead. Dries the surface for better bark, seasons all the way through.
- **Wet brine (use for lean poultry — whole chicken, turkey):** 1 cup kosher salt + ½ cup brown sugar per 1 gallon water. Submerge 8–12 hrs. See `brines-reference.md` for the dialed poultry brine.
- **Injection (use for brisket + pork butt):** get moisture deep into big cuts.
  - Beef: 1 cup low-sodium beef broth + 1 tbsp Worcestershire + 1 tsp of your rub, dissolved. Inject in a 1" grid.
  - Pork: 1 cup apple juice + ¼ cup apple cider vinegar + 2 tbsp brown sugar + 1 tbsp kosher salt.

### 2. Crust — the rub (right before it goes on)
Builds bark and color. Standard structure:
- **Base:** kosher salt + coarse black pepper (this alone = a Texas brisket).
- **Color + bark:** dark brown sugar + smoked paprika.
- **Character:** garlic powder, onion powder, chili powder / cayenne to taste.

**All-purpose BBQ rub** (scale up, keep in a shaker):
| Ingredient | Parts |
|---|---|
| Kosher salt | 2 |
| Coarse black pepper | 2 |
| Dark brown sugar | 2 |
| Smoked paprika | 1 |
| Garlic powder | 1 |
| Onion powder | 1 |
| Chili powder | ½ |
| Cayenne | ¼ |

- **Binder** (helps rub stick): a thin coat of **yellow mustard** or **hot sauce** — you won't taste it. Skip for beef if dry-brined; the surface moisture is enough.
- **Beef (brisket):** skip the sugar — salt + pepper (+ a little garlic) only. Sugar burns over a 12+ hr cook.

### 3. During — the spritz (after the bark sets, ~2 hrs in)
Keeps the surface moist, adds tang, helps smoke cling. Spritz every 45–60 min.
- **Pork/chicken:** equal parts apple cider vinegar + apple juice.
- **Beef:** equal parts apple cider vinegar + water (+ a splash of Worcestershire).

### 4. Finish — sauce or glaze (last 20–30 min, or at the table)
See `sauces.md`. Sugar-based sauces go on late or they scorch.

## Aromatics / prep-work cheat sheet (the exact one to use)
- **Onion:** **yellow onion** for cooking down (sauces, beans, chili) — sweetens when cooked. Red onion only when raw (garnish, slaw).
- **Garlic:** fresh minced when it cooks a while; garlic *powder* in rubs (fresh burns in a rub).
- **Vinegar:** **apple cider vinegar** is the BBQ default (tang + fruit). White vinegar only when you want pure sharp acid.
- **Sugar:** **dark brown sugar** for BBQ (molasses depth + color). White sugar only in brines where you don't want color.
- **Broth:** **low-sodium** always — you control the salt with the rub.
- **Paprika:** **smoked paprika** for BBQ, not sweet/plain.
- **Mustard binder:** **plain yellow mustard**, cheapest one.

## Timing (so nothing gets rushed)
| When | Do |
|---|---|
| Night before | Dry brine / wet brine, mix rub, make sauce |
| Morning of | Trim, inject, apply binder + rub, let sit 30–60 min while pit comes up |
| ~2 hrs in | Start spritzing |
| Last 30 min | Glaze/sauce if using |
| After pull | Rest (foil + cooler), 30 min–2 hrs depending on size |
